The Philosophy of Making Your Bed

Making your bed is the first thing you do once you’re up. That’s not just a fact, it’s the whole function. Some mornings you’ll get up, go to the bathroom, and climb straight back in, and I’m deeply guilty of this myself. But once the bed’s actually made, going back in stops feeling like an option. That alone is doing a lot of work before you’ve even had coffee.

There’s a hygiene case too. Opening up the covers lets everything air out properly, sweat and smells included, and if you can also open the blinds, sunlight is a genuinely natural disinfectant. Not clinical-grade cleaning, obviously, just the same logic behind hanging washing outside instead of leaving it balled up in a basket. Air and light do something a closed room can’t.

But the real reason, the one underneath all of that, is intention. You could get up, leave the sheets crumpled, and climb back into the same mess tonight anyway. Or you could take the extra thirty seconds and set the tone for the whole day before it’s even started. Getting into a bed that’s already made at the end of a long day feels like a small gift from someone who was thinking about you. That someone was you, this morning, being kind to tonight’s version of yourself.

If you don’t make your bed, I get why this sounds overhyped. It’s not really about the bed at all, though. It’s about the fact that the easy move is always to just get up, throw on whatever’s nearest, and go. Making the bed is the one small refusal of that easy move, right at the start of the day, before anything else has asked anything of you yet.

It was never about the bed.
